What's new

Every version of Friday, newest first: what's new, and what changed.

Friday 0.3.1

9 November 2026 · Download

Repeats you make work as they should, any Gmail with an app password, colours for events from the start, Friday on your phone, and many fixes from a full check of the app.

New

  • Friday on your phone over Tailscale: pair it with a QR code in Settings → Phone.
  • Read any Gmail with an app password, in Settings → Integrations → Gmail.
  • Events get colours from the start: Exam, Class, Meeting, Work, Exercise, Social and Travel, all yours to change.
  • The calendars on your Mac lead Settings → Integrations, and you can change one Setup answer without going through Setup again.
  • Keep a repeating task's times apart: "laundry at least 7 days apart".
  • A built-in preference to stay on one course for a stretch, and a switch for doing things in one go (Settings → Schedule).
  • A sentence with several times, like "no work 7:10–7:20 and 7:30–7:40", adds a rule for each; under any rule Friday read itself, "Not what you meant?" asks your AI.
  • "If math homework becomes overdue, it must be done before Sunday" is read as a rule.

Changed

  • Sleep and Meals are tabs of Insights.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do, in plain words.
  • The intro's town matches the season outside.
  • Double-clicking a planned task opens it in your to-dos.
  • Many more rules are read without the AI, and one Friday only half understands goes to the AI instead of being guessed.

Fixed

  • Events you make in Friday repeat as they should.
  • Switching off a repeat that came from Google no longer deletes it from Google.
  • Canvas and other calendar-link repeats with an end date come in, once, and aren't copied into Google.
  • Free/busy and all-day changes made in Google reach Friday.
  • Quick add reads "due 5pm fri", page ranges and times after midnight as meant, and exam prep by its title.
  • Work you've already timed today counts toward your daily limit; changing a task's length changes what's planned.
  • Security: other websites and connected assistants can no longer reach your AI key or your private settings.
  • Security updates for Google sign-in.

Friday 0.3.0

8 November 2026

Friday now lives at friday-scheduler.com, asks you less about your day, and gets birthdays and all-day events right.

New

  • Friday's site is friday-scheduler.com: the download, the Terminal install and updates all come from there, and downloads come from servers near you, so they're quicker.

Changed

  • With "Plan it again" on (Settings → Schedule), Friday no longer asks how much you did of work you didn't time: it already knows.
  • A timer stopped in under five minutes doesn't count as work: Friday says it was too short to count and keeps the task to do.
  • A new all-day event, like a birthday, leaves the day free for planning unless you say otherwise.

Fixed

  • Asking the AI to add a list of birthdays puts each one on the right day, whichever AI you use, and doesn't add one you already have.
  • Changing a repeating event from today on keeps its next date: yearly birthdays no longer all moved to today.
  • Deleting a repeating event from one time on removes those times from your calendar too.

Friday 0.2.0

7 November 2026

Friday now updates itself, asking you first and saying what changed.

New

  • When a new version is out, Friday asks the next time it opens, with what's new in it.
  • Update downloads it while you keep working, checks it's the version Friday's site published, and restarts into it.
  • The first time a new version opens, Friday shows what changed.
  • Settings → Updates: the version you have, Check now, and whether an update goes in right away or when you quit.

Friday 0.1.0

6 November 2026

The first version of Friday you can download.

New

  • Plans your week: tasks planned into your free time before they’re due, around your classes, work, meals and sleep, and planned again when things change.
  • Walk through your week: a first run through a pixel-art town that sets Friday up from your answers.
  • One list: everything you have to do by kind, with its time, when it’s planned and when it’s due.
  • Your Mac’s calendars and calendar feeds come in; events you make can go back to your calendar.
  • The Inbox, the review and rules in your own words: Friday asks only what it can’t know.
  • Ask the AI you choose, or none.
  • Everything runs on your Mac: no account, nothing to install besides the app.
